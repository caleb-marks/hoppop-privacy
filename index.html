HopPop Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 27, 2026

HopPop is a dictation app for iPhone. It turns your speech into text and comes with a keyboard so you can dictate in other apps. This policy explains what HopPop does with your voice, your words, and your device data.

Summary

  • Speech and Smart processing happen on your iPhone. HopPop never uploads your audio or text. Copying and sharing put your words where you choose; copies made in the app follow your iPhone's Handoff settings. Background copies stay on this iPhone and expire after one hour.
  • In-app Smart cleanup uses Apple Intelligence on supported iPhones. It never sends your text to a cloud model.
  • There is no account, analytics, advertising, tracking, or cloud sync. HopPop has no servers for your content.
  • HopPop goes online only to download its speech model files from huggingface.co and to check your unlock with Apple's App Store. Neither carries your speech or text.
  • Audio is deleted after transcribing, unless a recording needs recovery.
  • The App Store privacy label for HopPop is Data Not Collected.

What HopPop handles

Your voice. HopPop turns speech into text on your iPhone while you dictate. While a recording is unfinished, HopPop also copies it about once a second to a file on your iPhone, protected by iOS data protection and left out of backups. The file is deleted as soon as the recording's text is saved, or when you cancel or nothing was heard. If HopPop closes or the recording fails before it becomes text, if only part of it became text, or if your iPhone is too full to save the text safely, the file stays so HopPop can offer to turn it into text; you can delete it, and it is removed after 7 days. HopPop never keeps audio for history, analytics, or debugging. With AirPods that support high-quality recording, audio travels from the AirPods to your iPhone over Bluetooth and is handled there like any other microphone.

The mic for the keyboard. iOS doesn't let keyboards use the microphone, so the HopPop keyboard asks the HopPop app to record. During setup, enable HopPop's keyboard with Full Access and select it once. Tap the keyboard's mic: HopPop opens and records. After that, the keyboard's mic records right in your app while that session is ready. Opening HopPop by itself never turns on the microphone; to ready it without recording, tap Ready keyboard mic in HopPop. The iPhone may let the keyboard ask HopPop to open; if it does not, open HopPop from the Home Screen and tap Ready keyboard mic. The microphone stays ready for 15 minutes after the last recording, then turns off on its own. While it is ready, iOS shows the orange microphone dot. You can turn it off from HopPop's Live Activity on the Lock Screen (if Live Activities are on) or with the End Session control in Control Center. Between recordings HopPop turns nothing into text and saves no audio. It holds only the last third of a second of sound in memory, overwritten as it goes, so your first word isn't cut off when you tap the mic.

Words shown while you dictate. The Dictate screen and the keyboard show a rough preview of what you are saying. While you record, HopPop also keeps a copy of these words in protected storage on this iPhone, left out of backups, so a crash or a closed app doesn't lose them. The copy is deleted once the finished text is ready. If the finished text can't be made, these words are what HopPop keeps for you: they go to the app you were dictating into, or wait on a recovery card. For the keyboard, the preview also passes through the protected status file HopPop shares with its keyboard on this iPhone. It is cleared when the text is ready, when you cancel, when the mic turns off, or when HopPop next opens.

Your latest dictation. What you dictate in HopPop shows on the Dictate screen, where you can copy, share, or clear it. Earlier versions of HopPop had Notes; any notes you saved there stay in HopPop's protected storage on this iPhone. Settings › Your old notes shows them, read-only, to copy, share, or delete. Deleting HopPop also removes them.

History. On a new install History is on and keeps your text for 7 days. One switch in Settings turns it off. It holds up to your newest 500 dictations (or 5 MB of text); older ones drop off. History stores text only, is left out of backups, and is never sent or synced. An earlier installation may keep its saved 30-day or until-deleted setting; the same storage limits still apply. Turning History off deletes what it holds. Turning it back on uses 7 days.

Recovery copies. So your words aren't lost, HopPop keeps a copy of each dictation on this iPhone, left out of backups, even with History off. The copy is deleted about a day after the words arrive in their app, or after you copy, share, or clear them on the Dictate screen (with History on, History keeps them instead). Words dictated in HopPop that you leave on screen are kept for at most 7 days, the newest 5 only. Words that never arrive wait on a recovery card until you send, copy, or discard them; HopPop never deletes them on its own. A damaged recovery file that HopPop can't read is deleted after 7 days.

Dictionary. Words you add, including corrections you choose to teach it, stay on this iPhone and help HopPop spell them right. The HopPop keyboard gets a copy so it doesn't autocorrect them. “Learn from corrections” is on until you turn it off. HopPop never watches what you type in other apps to learn.

Keyboard typing. The HopPop keyboard corrects spelling with Apple's spelling checker on your iPhone. When you undo an autocorrection, the keyboard remembers the word you put back so it doesn't change it again. Those words stay on this iPhone only. iOS also provides supplementary keyboard words, which may include contact names and text replacements. The keyboard uses them to leave names alone and expand your shortcuts; it does not save or send that list.

Contact names. Optional, and off until you turn on Use contact names in Dictionary and allow Contacts. HopPop then reads only the names of the contacts you allow, so it spells them right when you dictate. It keeps those names in memory only: it never saves them to your Dictionary, the keyboard, or disk, and never sends them anywhere. Turn it off in Dictionary at any time.

Voice commands. Phrases such as “new paragraph”, “scratch that”, or “delete last word” are recognized by a fixed word list on your iPhone. The keyboard only edits text it can see in the field you are typing in.

Smart. In HopPop, Smart can clean up a dictation (fillers, false starts, grammar) with Apple Intelligence on a supported iPhone where it is enabled. Original brings your exact words back. If cleanup is unavailable or cannot finish, your original words stay. Apple Intelligence runs on your iPhone; HopPop does not send your text to a cloud model. Earlier versions let you add a cloud model with an AI key; that option is gone, and HopPop deletes any key it saved.

Settings. Your preferences, and simple counts such as how many words you have dictated, stay on this iPhone. Dictionary words, settings, and notes from an earlier version may be included in your iPhone backup. HopPop does not sync them.

Crash and performance reports. HopPop has no crash-reporting or analytics service. If you turn on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings (Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements), Apple shares crash reports and app usage statistics with the developer under Apple's own privacy policy. They describe what went wrong in the app, not what you said or wrote. iOS also gives HopPop short performance and crash reports each day. HopPop keeps the newest ones on this iPhone and never sends them anywhere.

Purchases. Your first 100 dictations are free. HopPop counts them on this iPhone (in its Keychain, so deleting and reinstalling HopPop doesn't reset the count; the count is never sent anywhere). After 100, dictation stops until you unlock HopPop once for $19.99 (the App Store shows the price for your country). Typing and History keep working. There is no subscription, and nothing is charged on its own. Apple handles every payment; HopPop never sees your payment details. On this iPhone, HopPop stores only whether you unlocked it and how many free dictations you used, and shares that with its keyboard so the keyboard can tell without going online.

Keyboard Full Access

The HopPop keyboard types without Full Access. To dictate from the keyboard, iOS needs you to turn on Allow Full Access, because the keyboard and the app pass your words to each other through a private shared container on this iPhone. The keyboard never records audio and never goes online. iOS warns that keyboards with Full Access “can access what you type”. HopPop uses that access only on this iPhone: to share your words and settings between the keyboard and the app, and to make keys tap back when you type. If the keyboard can't open HopPop, open it yourself and tap Ready keyboard mic; manage your unlock in HopPop too.

Network

HopPop itself goes online for only these things:

  • The speech download. HopPop's speech files (about 632 MB, downloaded once) come from huggingface.co, a public host for open speech models. HopPop asks before that download starts (Wi-Fi Only, Use Cellular, or Later). Until you allow it — or while it runs — HopPop uses your iPhone's own on-device speech recognition. HopPop uses fixed addresses and checks each file's SHA-256 fingerprint before using it. Like any download, the host sees your iPhone's internet address; the request carries no speech, text, or account.
  • The App Store. Apple handles purchases and restores, and HopPop checks the verified purchase status. HopPop does not send Apple your speech or text.

Until HopPop's speech is downloaded, HopPop can use your iPhone's own on-device speech recognition instead. If its files for your language are missing, iOS downloads them from Apple. That download carries no speech or text, and your audio still stays on this iPhone.

After the download, dictation works offline. You can check: turn on Airplane Mode and dictate. HopPop never sends your audio or transcripts anywhere. Your words go into the app you dictate in, or wherever you copy or share them. Background copies stay on this iPhone and expire after one hour. Copies made in the app use the system clipboard. If Universal Clipboard is enabled through Handoff, iOS may make copied text available on your other Apple devices under your system settings. This is separate from HopPop, which has no cloud sync.

What we do not collect

HopPop does not create accounts, sell data, show ads, charge a subscription, or run analytics, tracking, or crash-reporting services. There are no HopPop servers for your speech or text, and none for Smart. The App Store privacy label for HopPop is Data Not Collected: HopPop never receives your data.

Your controls

  • Turn History off in Settings (this deletes what it holds).
  • Turn off “Learn from corrections” in Settings.
  • Clear your latest dictation, delete Dictionary words, and clear History in the app.
  • Clear the words the keyboard learned in Settings › Typing.
  • Turn off the ready mic from the keyboard (End), the Lock Screen, or Control Center.
  • Turn off microphone access for HopPop in iOS Settings.
  • Turn off Full Access or remove the HopPop keyboard in iOS Settings.
  • Delete HopPop to remove its files from this iPhone. The free-dictation count in Keychain remains so reinstalling does not reset the free allowance.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears here and in the app with a new date.
